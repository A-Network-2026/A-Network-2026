## 6. On the honest stance

You may worry that "no compensation" will mean no engagement. A few
honest counterpoints:

- **A small lie about compensation is worse than the truth.** Posting
  "100,000 wANET bounty!" and then negotiating the payout down (or
  delaying it indefinitely because the vault signers are slow) burns
  far more credibility than offering nothing and delivering nothing.
- **Recognition compounds.** A researcher whose name lives in the
  whitepaper changelog forever, attached to a real finding on a real
  contract holding real assets, gets a permanent line on their CV.
  That has measurable career value.
- **Self-selection is a feature.** A program that explicitly says "no
  money" filters out the bottom-of-funnel low-effort submissions
  that paid bounties drown in. The researchers who show up are the
  ones who care about the work.
- **The door is not closed forever.** Once the project has a real
  revenue line (audit-grade external review of fee flows, etc.),
  compensation can be added later. Adding compensation to a
  recognition program is easy. Withdrawing compensation from a paid
  program once promised is a credibility disaster.

This is the path. It is harder. It is also defensible.
