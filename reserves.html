METHOD AND LIMITS

How to read this page. Balances come from eth_call / eth_getBalance on BNB Chain and Ethereum, getBalance / getTokenAccountsByOwner on Solana, the Blockstream Esplora API for Bitcoin, and the public A Network Layer 1 API. Revenue is reconstructed from ERC-20 Transfer events whose recipient is the treasury; the sender of each transfer is shown so you can distinguish interface fees from other deposits. USD values use the live DexScreener and CoinGecko prices at the time you load the page, not historical prices.

Which node answers. Reads go to the project's archive RPC endpoint first and fail over automatically to public BNB Chain, Ethereum and Solana nodes when a provider cannot be reached from your network (the RPC line at the top shows the node that actually answered). Current balances and contract state are served by any node. The event history behind the fee ledger and the release list needs an archive node; when none is reachable those two items are shown as unavailable rather than guessed, and the BscScan links let you verify them by hand.

What this page proves and what it does not. It proves what the published addresses hold and what entered them, at the moment of your request. It does not prove who controls the keys, and it is not an audit opinion or a financial attestation. Native-coin (BNB, ETH) inflows cannot be enumerated from an RPC node without an indexer, so only the current native balances are shown. Operating expenses paid out of the treasury are visible as outflows on the explorers linked above.

Layer 1 export liabilities. The Layer 1 export ledger (/bridge/burns) is behind the operator switch described in whitepaper Version 4.6. While it is switched off the page reconciles from the BNB Chain side only: vault balance plus total released equals the wANET escrowed, and the number of release events on BNB Chain is the number of exports ever honoured. When the switch is on, the page additionally compares pending Layer 1 burns with releases.
